# Case Study Content

## Goal

The case study should prove product judgment, but it also needs to describe the product honestly.

Ghostwriter is a fun, comforting app. It is not a productivity tool, a writing assistant, or a work utility.

Keep the page short, visual, specific, and plainspoken.

## Route

Recommended route:

- `/ghostwriter/case-study`

## Page Structure

### 1. Hero

Title:

`Ghostwriter`

Subtitle:

`Ghostwriter was never meant to help with work. I built it as a fun, comforting app: something you open to play with a sentence, try on a mood, and watch language come back a little softer, stranger, or sweeter.`

Quick facts row:

- `Role: product, design, engineering`
- `Intent: playful, comforting, low-stakes`
- `Stack: Next.js, React, Groq-compatible API, Supabase`

### 2. The Idea

Suggested copy:

`The point was never productivity. The point was comfort, play, and a little bit of delight. Ghostwriter is something you open to play with a sentence, try on a mood, and enjoy the rewrite.`

### 3. How It Works

Keep this section very short. Three cards is enough:

- `Pick a voice`
- `Turn the dial`
- `Watch it change`

The copy should stay focused on feeling, not mechanics-heavy detail.

### 4. Why This Stack

Add a dedicated stack section with short explanations:

- `Next.js`: main product shell, route structure, share pages, API surface
- `React`: interaction model for the dial, playback, and UI state
- `Groq-compatible API`: simple rewrite layer that keeps the app focused
- `Supabase`: lightweight persistence for saved rewrites and links

## Supporting Card

Suggested title:

`Why this project matters to me`

Suggested copy:

`I like products that know what they are. Ghostwriter is small on purpose, and that restraint is part of the design.`

## Tone Guidelines

- first person singular is fine
- lead with mood and feeling, not utility
- use concrete examples from the product instead of abstract claims
- prefer simple sentences over "writerly" ones
- keep the page short enough to skim in one pass
- avoid MBA language and generic "AI productivity" framing
- explain trade-offs clearly
- show intent and restraint
- do not invent user research, quotes, or metrics that do not exist

## Case Study Review Checklist

1. Does the page begin with a human problem readers instantly recognize?
2. Does it make the technical decisions feel like answers to that problem?
3. Does it show product taste, not just features?
4. Does it avoid invented proof points, sterile portfolio language, or lines that sound over-written?
5. Is it short enough to read quickly while still feeling specific?
